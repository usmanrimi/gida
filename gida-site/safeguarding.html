Prosperity cannot exist where people are exploited.

GIDA enforces a zero-tolerance policy against gender-based discrimination, sexual exploitation and abuse (PSEAH), child abuse, human rights violations, and extractive development practices, binding on all personnel and partners.

Policy mandate

Document GIDA-POL-2026-001 (Rev. 2)

Effective July 2026. This framework legally and operationally binds all GIDA personnel and external partners across agricultural, off-farm enterprise, digital, health/WASH, and green infrastructure initiatives.

Compliance standards

Aligned to international frameworks

UN/IASC PSEAH Core Principles · World Bank Environmental and Social Frameworks · FCDO Core Standards · Localized Non-Extractive Development Frameworks.

Core policy pillars

Five pillars protect the people we work with and for.

01

Inclusive Rural Systems & GESI

  • Ownership over attendance, programming must ensure target groups acquire tangible ownership of productive assets, tools, and governance roles, not just headcounts.
  • Broad rural scope, GESI integration applies equally across value chains, micro-enterprises, clean energy guilds, and water/health enterprises.
  • Intersecting vulnerabilities, project design accommodates the specific barriers faced by young mothers, unbanked household heads, rural youth, and PwDs.
02

Protection from Sexual Exploitation, Abuse & Harassment (PSEAH)

  • Absolute prohibition, exchanging money, employment, goods, inputs, water access, or project services for sexual favors is strictly forbidden.
  • Ban on coercive power relationships, sexual relationships between GIDA staff/contractors and beneficiaries or vulnerable community members are strictly prohibited.
  • Safe physical & digital delivery, all field activities and distribution points must operate in physically safe, neutral, accessible venues.
03

Child Safeguarding & Protection of Minors (Under 18 Years)

  • Absolute protection, no staff member, consultant, or field partner may engage in child labor, physical punishment, or unmonitored contact with minors.
  • Youth enterprise safeguards, young participants in apprentice or green-skills training are protected from hazardous environments, financial exploitation, or safety hazards.
04

Dignified Communications, Media & Data Ethics

  • Informed consent, photography, video, or personal story collection requires voluntary written or recorded verbal consent via GIDA's official Informed Consent Form.
  • Dignified representation, all media must portray community members with agency, dignity, and respect.
  • Strict minor photography protocol, minors may only be photographed with explicit written guardian consent; images must never disclose identifying or location data.
05

Anti-Extractive Development

  • Fair value & data equity, community members are never treated as passive data sources; insights gathered are fed back in actionable, accessible ways.
  • Asset & local value retention, every initiative leaves tangible, locally owned physical, financial, or technical assets in the community at closeout.
  • Valuing rural time, participation in co-design or field trials is respected and appropriately compensated.
  • Zero "aid-washing", GIDA forbids performative projects designed solely for institutional overhead or donor photo-ops without long-term sustainability plans.

Community-Based Grievance Redress Mechanism

A hybrid reporting system, built for low-connectivity rural settings.

Digital & direct channels

  • Dedicated email

    safeguarding@gida-action.org

  • Confidential hotline / WhatsApp line

    Managed directly by the GESI Lead.

Community-based (offline)

  • Physical confidential boxes

    Located at local partner hubs and kiosks.

  • Designated community focal points

    Local female and youth focal points trained to receive reports.

Mandatory escalation timelines

Strict international response windows, every time.

Within 24 hours

Initial logging & incident triage

Handled by the Safeguarding Focal Person / Legal Officer.

Immediate, within 24 hours

Survivor medical & support referral

Coordinated by the Field Protection Lead.

Within 48 hours

Executive & Board notification

Escalated to the Lead – Inclusive Development and CEO.

Within 72 hours

Donor / funder notification

Per contractual mandate, led by Executive Leadership.

Within 30 days

Independent investigation completed

Conducted by the Independent Inquiry Panel.

Survivor & community first: the physical safety, mental health, privacy, economic dignity, and informed choice of survivors and community members remain paramount throughout all reporting and inquiry phases.

Institutional compliance & due diligence

Vetting, contracts, and annual review.

  • Employee vetting

    Mandatory background and reference checks for all staff, consultants, and field enumerators prior to engagement.

  • Binding partner addendum

    All private sector partners, green construction contractors, sub-grantees, and research partners must sign the GIDA Safeguarding Code of Conduct Addendum as a binding condition of contract execution.

  • Annual policy audit

    Reviewed annually by the Lead – Inclusive Development to maintain compliance with evolving donor regulations (World Bank, FCDO, UN agencies, AGRA, and global foundations).
